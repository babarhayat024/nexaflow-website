POS and invoicing software for shops in Pakistan: what to look for

A checklist for shop owners choosing billing software, from the counter to the daily cash closing, including what FBR asks of large retailers.

By Babar Hayat, founder of NexaFlow · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

A shop in Pakistan needs software that makes a bill in seconds, scans barcodes, keeps stock up to date, tracks credit customers and closes the day's cash. A single small shop can start with simple POS and invoicing software. A shop with several counters or branches, or one that also sells wholesale, is better with POS that is connected to stock and accounts. Large Tier-1 retailers must also have their POS connected to the FBR system.

POS software and invoicing software: what is the difference?

POS (point of sale) software is used at the counter. It makes a bill quickly, takes cash, card or wallet payments and prints a receipt. Invoicing software makes proper invoices for customers who buy on credit, such as other shops or companies, and keeps track of what they owe. Many shops need both: POS for walk-in customers, invoices for wholesale and credit customers.

What to look for

  • Fast billing with barcodes. Scan, change quantity, print. A busy counter cannot wait.
  • Stock that updates by itself. Every sale reduces stock, every purchase adds to it, so you know what to reorder.
  • Units and packs. Sell in pieces, dozens or cartons, with the right price for each.
  • Credit customers (udhaar). A ledger for each customer, with what they owe and when they paid.
  • Daily closing. Cash in the drawer against sales in the system, with the difference shown.
  • Sales by cashier. Who sold what, and which counter is short.
  • Works when the internet is down. Or at least a clear plan for what happens then.
  • Reports on your phone. Today's sales, top products and profit, without going to the shop.
  • More than one branch. If you plan to open a second shop, choose software that already handles branches.

FBR and Tier-1 retailers

Pakistan's tax law requires Tier-1 retailers, such as large stores, chain outlets and some restaurants, to connect their point of sale with the FBR's computerised system, so that each bill is reported in real time and gets an FBR invoice number. This integration has to be done through software that meets FBR's rules.

Most small shops are not Tier-1 retailers. If you are not sure which group you are in, ask your tax adviser before choosing software, and check the official FBR website.

Common mistakes

  • Buying software before entering your products with correct barcodes, units and prices.
  • Starting without counting stock first, so the system is wrong from day one.
  • Giving every cashier the owner's login, so nobody knows who changed a price.
  • Keeping credit customers in a notebook next to the software.

When a shop needs more than POS

If you also supply other shops, have order bookers, or buy and sell in large volumes, POS alone is not enough. You need sales orders, delivery, stock across locations and proper accounts. That is where an ERP helps. Read ERP vs accounting software to see the difference.

Questions people ask

Is there free POS software for a small shop in Pakistan?

There are free and low-cost options. Check three things before you choose: whether you can export your data, whether it handles your units and credit customers, and who helps you when something goes wrong.

Can POS software work on a mobile phone?

Yes. Many systems run on an Android phone or tablet with a small printer. For a busy counter, a computer with a barcode scanner is usually faster.
