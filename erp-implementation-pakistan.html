ERP implementation in Pakistan: how long it takes and what it costs

Before you start an ERP project, know what the money is spent on and where the time goes. This guide explains both, for businesses in Pakistan.

By Babar Hayat, founder of NexaFlow · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

A small business that needs sales, stock and invoicing can usually go live on an ERP in a few weeks. A distributor with several warehouses and order bookers usually takes one to three months. A manufacturer with production and costing often needs three to six months. The cost has several parts: the software licence or subscription, the setup work, moving your data, training and support. A good implementer gives you the full price in writing, in rupees, before starting.

The parts of the cost

  • Software licence or subscription. Most ERPs charge per user per month, often in dollars. Some have a free version that you host yourself.
  • Who needs a login. This is where Pakistani businesses lose money. If every foreman, store keeper and order booker needs a full licence, the monthly bill grows fast, so most of them stay on paper. Ask before you buy whether floor and field staff can have limited, low-cost accounts.
  • Hosting. Cloud hosting is paid monthly. Your own server is paid once, but someone must keep it updated and backed up.
  • Setup work. Products, units and cartons, recipes, price lists, warehouses, taxes, the chart of accounts, approvals and print layouts.
  • Moving your data. Products, customers, suppliers, opening stock and opening balances. Clean data makes this fast; messy Excel sheets make it slow.
  • Training. Each person learns their own screens: foreman, order booker, warehouse, accountant, owner.
  • Custom work. New reports, special print layouts or new features. This is where costs grow, so keep it for what you really need.
  • Support after go-live. The first month always brings questions. Agree in advance who answers them.

How the time is spent

StageWhat happens
1. UnderstandWalk through how an order, a purchase, a production run and a payment move in your business today.
2. PlanA written plan: which parts, which settings, what data moves, who is trained, and the price.
3. ConfigureSet up the system with your real products, units, recipes, price lists, warehouses and taxes.
4. Move dataImport products, customers, suppliers, opening stock and opening balances.
5. TestRun real work from start to finish: order, production, delivery, invoice, payment, report.
6. Train and go liveTrain each team on its own screens, then switch on a chosen date, usually the first of a month.
7. SupportFix small problems in the first weeks and add what was left for later.

What makes an ERP project slow and expensive

  • No one inside the business owns the project or has time to answer questions.
  • Product codes, units and customer names are different in every Excel sheet.
  • Trying to copy the old way exactly instead of using the software's standard way.
  • Asking for many custom reports before the basics are working.
  • Changing the plan halfway through.

How to keep the cost down

  • Start with sales, stock and accounts. Add production or payroll after the base is stable.
  • Use standard features first, then settings, and custom code only as the last step.
  • Clean your product list and customer list before the project starts.
  • Choose one person in your business who decides and signs off each stage.
  • Give floor and field staff limited accounts instead of full licences.

Things to set up for Pakistan

  • Rupee as the company currency, and your NTN and STRN on the company and invoices.
  • Sales tax and withholding tax rates as they apply to your business. Confirm these with your tax adviser before going live, because they change from time to time.
  • Units and cartons that match how you buy and sell, for example cartons of different sizes for different products.
  • Price lists for different customer groups, such as wholesale and retail.
  • Credit limits, cheques and customer ledgers that match how you collect payments.

Questions people ask

Can I use free ERP software?

Yes, some ERPs have a free version, but you still pay for a server and for someone to install, update and back it up. Check that the parts you need, such as manufacturing or payroll, are included in the free version.

Do I need an official partner of the software company?

No. What matters is experience with businesses like yours, a written plan, clear pricing and support after go-live.

Can my field staff use a mobile app that connects to our ERP?

Yes, if your ERP allows outside connections through an API key. We build order booking and field apps that send their data into the system you already use. See our services.

Our ERP is set up but does not work well. Can it be fixed?

Usually, yes. Most problems come from settings, product and unit setup or opening balances, not from the software itself. A review shows what to fix first.
