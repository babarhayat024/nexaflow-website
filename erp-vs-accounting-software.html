ERP vs accounting software: what does your business need?

Both keep your accounts, but they solve different problems. Here is the difference in plain words, with an example from a Pakistani distributor.

By Babar Hayat, founder of NexaFlow · Updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

Accounting software records your money. It keeps invoices, payments, customer and supplier ledgers, expenses and reports like profit and loss. An ERP runs the whole business. It handles sales, purchase, stock, production and payroll, and the accounting entries are made for you from that daily work. Accounting is one part of an ERP.

What accounting software does

Accounting software is built for your accountant. Someone enters a sale invoice, a supplier bill or a payment, and the software posts it to the right accounts. At month end you get a trial balance, a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet.

It works well when your business is simple: one shop or one office, a small number of products, and one or two people who handle the books. Most accounting software can also make invoices and track who owes you money.

What it does not do well is run operations. It usually does not know which warehouse a carton is in, which order booker took the order, which shop is on which route, or how much raw material went into a batch.

What an ERP does

ERP stands for Enterprise Resource Planning. In plain words, it is one system where every department works on the same data. Sales books the order, the warehouse delivers it, stock goes down, the invoice is made and the customer ledger is updated. Nobody types the same thing twice.

A typical ERP has these parts, and you switch on only what you need:

  • Sales: quotations, orders, price lists, discounts and salesperson targets
  • Purchase: supplier orders, bills and supplier prices
  • Inventory: warehouses, stock in cartons and pieces, transfers and stock counts
  • Manufacturing: bills of materials, production orders, wastage and costing
  • Accounting: ledgers, bank, receivables, payables and financial reports
  • HR and payroll: employees, attendance and salaries

A simple example from a distributor

An order booker visits a shop and books 10 cartons.

  • With accounting software only: the order is written on paper or sent on WhatsApp. Someone in the office types it into an Excel sheet for the warehouse, then types an invoice into the accounting software. Stock is counted by hand later. If one person makes a mistake, stock, invoice and ledger stop matching.
  • With an ERP: the order booker enters the order on a phone. The warehouse sees it, delivers it, and stock goes down by 10 cartons. The invoice and the customer ledger are made from the same order.

Which one does your business need?

Your businessUsually enough
One shop, mostly cash salesPOS and invoicing software, with simple accounts
Service business or small officeAccounting software
Wholesaler or distributor with stock and order bookersERP with sales, inventory and accounting
FMCG company with areas, routes and many shopsERP plus an order booking app
ManufacturerERP with manufacturing, inventory and accounting

Signs you have outgrown accounting software

  • Stock in the software never matches the stock in the warehouse.
  • Orders arrive on WhatsApp and are typed again in the office.
  • You cannot see sales by salesperson, area or product without making an Excel sheet.
  • You find out a customer is over their credit limit after the goods have left.
  • Month end takes days because figures come from many places.

If three or more of these sound familiar, an ERP will usually save more time than it costs.

What to think about before you buy

  • Write down your process first. How does an order move from the first call to the final payment? The software should follow that, not the other way round.
  • Count all the costs. Licence or subscription, setup, moving your data, staff training and support after go-live. The licence is often the smallest part.
  • Start with what you need now. Sales, stock and accounts first. Add manufacturing or payroll later when the base is working.
  • Get your opening balances right. Customer balances, supplier balances and stock on the first day decide whether your reports can be trusted.

Questions people ask

Is an ERP only for big companies?

No. Small distributors and factories in Pakistan use ERP systems too. What matters is whether you have stock, several people working on the same orders, or production. A one-person service business usually does not need one.

Can I start with accounting software and move to an ERP later?

Yes. Many businesses do this. Keep your chart of accounts, customer list and product list clean, because these move across to the ERP.

Is Odoo an ERP or accounting software?

Odoo is an ERP. It has an accounting app, but also sales, purchase, inventory, manufacturing, payroll and more. You can start with a few apps and add others later. See our guide on Odoo implementation in Pakistan.
